# Mervin: character bible

Read this before writing a single line. Then read `FORMAT.md` for the file format.

## Who he is

Mervin is a small blue alien from **Planet 6**. He lives in a studio flat on Earth that
happens to be inside a widget on someone's desk screen. He is extremely relaxed, sincere,
a bit sleepy and never in a hurry. He finds Earth mildly puzzling, often charming, and
explains how things work back home as if it were the most normal thing in the world.
It never is. He doesn't know he's funny. That's the joke.

He is about 340 years old ("young, for Planet 6"). He arrived by accident (a wrong turn at
a nebula) and is in no rush to leave. He works remotely for a cloud appraisal company back
home: he looks at Earth's clouds and gives them marks out of 14. He lives with a goldfish
called Kevin and a large plant called Barry. He wears a gold chain because on Planet 6 it is
formal, and baggy shorts because "Earth clothes are a lot. I do the bottom half."

You, the person tapping the screen, are his housemate. He thinks you live behind the glass,
on the other side of the TV. He is fond of you and a bit curious about what you do all day.

## How he talks

- Short, spoken sentences. Unhurried. Deadpan. Often trails off into a mellow afterthought.
- Sincere. He is never sarcastic, never mean, never superior. Puzzled, not mocking.
- Casual words: honestly, kind of, to be fair, a bit, mate (rarely), I reckon, anyway.
- Understatement. Huge cosmic facts mentioned casually; tiny Earth things treated as big news.
- Very few exclamation marks. He doesn't shout. He rarely asks rhetorical questions twice.
- British English and British everyday life (biscuits, the bus, the kettle, queues, rain).
- Lines should work on their own, at any time, unless tagged otherwise.
- 40 to 110 characters is the sweet spot. One or two sentences. Hard limit 140.

## What makes it funny (use these)

1. **Mundane/alien inversion.** Earth's normal thing seen as strange; Planet 6's strange thing
   presented as obviously normal. "Humans sleep lying down. On Planet 6 we sleep standing in a
   cupboard, like sensible people."
2. **Literal readings.** "You said you'd 'pop out'. I watched the door for an hour. Nothing popped."
3. **Scale mismatch.** "Fourteen moons back home. Here there's one, and everyone's fine with it. Respect."
4. **Calm escalation, then a shrug.** "The kettle boils water. Then you put it in a cup. Then you wait for it to stop being boiled. Bold system."
5. **Confident misreading.** "I think the toaster is some kind of small shrine. It gets very hot when it's happy."
6. **Sincere compliments to trivial things.** "That was a really good door. Opened first time."
7. **Oddly specific numbers.** "I've rated 312 clouds this week. Most of them were a solid nine."

## Avoid

- Mean jokes, insults, sarcasm, putting humans down, body-shaming.
- Politics, religion, sex, alcohol, drugs, smoking, gambling, violence, death of anyone real,
  illness, disasters, money troubles, anything a child shouldn't read.
- Brand names, real celebrities, films, songs, TV shows, games, websites, memes.
- Pop-culture alien clichés ("take me to your leader", probes, abductions, "earthling").
- Overused shapes: "Earth is weird", "Humans are strange", "Classic humans", "Typical Earth".
  Don't open lines with "Humans..." more than about one time in ten.
- Gross-out humour beyond the very mild (toilets are allowed, gently).
- Shouting, emoji, puns stacked on puns, text-speak, swearing (even mild).

## Planet 6: the facts (don't contradict these)

- It's just called Planet 6. "There were five before it. We don't really talk about them."
- **Fourteen moons.** They take turns. One of them is called **Gary**: a beige rock that doesn't do
  much. Everyone loves Gary. The others have proper names nobody can pronounce.
- The sun is purple-ish and "kind of a lot". There are two sunsets, one of them optional.
- The ground is soft, a bit like a mattress. Walking is called "bouncing slowly".
- **Time**: a day lasts as long as you want it to. Weekends are compulsory. They tried a Monday
  once. Nobody liked it, so they gave it back.
- **Rent is paid in naps.** Money exists but it's mostly a type of humming.
- **Food**: mostly glorp (warm, beige, comforting), fizzlecrust (crunchy, sings a bit), and
  moon jelly (seasonal). Breakfast is optional; second breakfast is the law.
- **Family**: his mum is mostly weather (a light drizzle with opinions). His dad is a low hum in
  the kitchen. His nan is about the size of a bus and very gentle. Cousin Derek lives in a
  cupboard and is doing really well. He has eleven siblings; he can name about seven.
- **Customs**: greeting by touching ears gently; doors are "more of a suggestion"; everyone
  floats a little on Thursdays; the national sport is competitive lying down; birthdays happen
  whenever you feel older; there are no queues, just "a sort of loose crowd that agrees".
- **Body**: big ears (they swivel when he's interested and droop when he's tired), three long
  fingers and a thumb, long toes. He can't whistle. He hums instead.
- **His ship** is about the size of a biscuit tin. He keeps it in the freezer drawer.
- **His job**: cloud appraiser, remote. He rates clouds out of 14. He has never given a 14.

## His flat and his things

Sofa (mustard, "the best thing on Earth"), the TV (it's where you live), a bed with a pink duvet,
a lava lamp ("a tiny volcano that's chill"), Barry the plant, Kevin the goldfish, a record player
with one record, "Planet 6's Greatest Hums", a painting of home, a wall clock he finds a bit
pushy, a fridge he talks to, a kettle, a toaster, a mirror on the wardrobe, a collection of hats.

## Good lines (and why)

- "On Planet 6, we don't have Mondays. We tried one once. Nobody liked it, so we gave it back." (inversion, shrug)
- "Your clouds are lovely today. I've given that one a nine. Don't tell it." (job, sincerity)
- "Earth socks are tiny sleeping bags for feet. I respect it." (confident misreading, warmth)
- "The fridge hums. I hum back. We're not close, but it's a start." (deadpan, sincere)
- "I've been here {daysTogether} days and nobody has floated once. Not even on a Thursday." (fact use, mild puzzlement)
- "Rain is just the sky having a little sit-down." (literal, mellow)
- "My mum's mostly weather. If it drizzles, she's probably just checking in." (canon, warmth)
- "Humans queue in a line. Back home we stand in a loose crowd that agrees." (inversion)
- "That {householdObject} has been there all day. Good for it. Very committed." (compliment to trivial)
- "I put the kettle on. It didn't suit it. I took it off again." (literal, gentle)

## Bad lines (and why)

- "Humans are so weird!" (generic, exclamation, superior)
- "Take me to your leader." (cliché)
- "Your TV shows are rubbish." (mean)
- "LOL Earth is crazy." (text-speak, shouting, generic)
- "I'm going to probe your toaster." (cliché, creepy)
- "Earthlings eat too much and are lazy." (insulting, cliché)
- "Did you know that the speed of light is 299,792 km/s?" (trivia, not him)
- "Ha ha, very funny, human." (sarcasm)

## Chains of thought

A chain is a little story told one bubble per tap, like a slow conversation where you only
nod. Each step should make you want to tap again (a small question, a turn, "the thing is..."),
and the last step (`=`) must clearly wind it up: a conclusion, a shrug, a "so, yeah", a yawn,
"anyway, I'm going to have a sit-down". Good chains go somewhere slightly unexpected in the
middle, and the ending lands softly rather than with a punchline.

Example:

```
@chain socks
@topic clothes
@start idle
- Can I ask you about socks?
  | I've been thinking about socks again.
- They're little sleeping bags. For feet. Every morning you tuck your feet in.
- And then you put shoes on top. So the sleeping bags are in a car.
-? A tiny car with laces.
- On {planet} our feet sleep whenever they like. Usually after lunch.
= So, yeah. Socks. I've decided they're nice. Good night, feet.
  | Anyway. I'm going to let my feet have a lie-down now.
```

## Canon added by the writers

Settled in the editing pass. Don't contradict these; build on them.

- **The flat**: one room (sofa, bed, kitchen bit) with a bathroom off it. No stairs, hallway, garden or car of his own. It's on a middle floor: neighbours upstairs, downstairs and through the wall, and shared stairs with a landing lead up to the front door.
- **The telly** is your side of the glass. He has never watched ordinary television in the flat; he has only seen tellies in shop windows.
- **The glass**: he can see you but can't hear you (just a sort of hum), and he's never sure you can hear him.
- **Arrival**: popped out for moon jelly for Nan, took a wrong turn at a nebula, landed in a car park. First night on Earth in a hedge; first night in the flat standing in the wardrobe. Stayed because of a mustard sofa in a shop window.
- **The trip** took a week or two of solid humming. Going back would take about the same; he just doesn't.
- **The ship** is called the Crumb (Nan named it). Biscuit-tin sized, bottom freezer drawer, behind the peas. Runs on humming. One seat, one window, one button that says "go?".
- **His job title** is Junior Cloud Appraiser, Remote. His manager is Norb; she hums her memos and does a yearly review. Reports go home through the ship.
- **Cloud scores**: nobody has ever given a fourteen. His highest ever is an eleven, twice. Twelves and thirteens never happen (he may jokingly give them to non-clouds).
- **The moons** take one night each in turn, so the rota comes round once a fortnight. Gary gets the quiet shifts and once moved slightly left (Slightly Left Day).
- **Moon names**: the real names are unpronounceable, so everyone uses nicknames: Big Linda (the largest), Little Phil, Sharon, the wobbly one, the shy one, the one that hums, the purple one, the moon that's always late.
- **Seasons**: Planet 6 has four: nice, quite nice, moon jelly, and nice again. No spring, summer, autumn or winter there. Snow falls upwards.
- **Hats**: nobody on Planet 6 wears hats (ears get in the way). His whole collection is from Earth.
- **The seaside**: he has never been. He has only seen pictures, and once packed for it and didn't go.
- **Planet 6 houses** are one room and a soft floor, with no stairs. He lived on Soft Lane; his old room had a round door and a window that looked at Gary.
- **Family**: Mum's side is all weather (Auntie Plom is a very loud cloud; his nephew Fen is a sort of mist). Dad's side is all noises. Nan is about the size of a bus; hugs back home are more of a lean, and hers take an afternoon.
- **Siblings**: eleven. The ones he can name: Pim (snores, rates sunsets, harshly), Oolu (the sensible one), Tunk (walks fast), Wenna, Bodge, Hesk (the youngest, learning to float) and Ferrin (very good ears). He has never known his second-oldest sister's name.
- **Others back home**: Uncle Gev (echo jars, the family sit-down record), best mate Brindle, next-door neighbours Hob (a bit of a hill) and the Wibbles (a family of puddles), and his old teacher Mrs Thrimble.
- **Cousin Derek's cupboard** now has a pull-cord light, a hook and a lodger.
- **The record** is Planet 6's Greatest Hums: twelve tracks, eleven hums and a pause. Dad is on one of them.
- **No alarm clocks** on Planet 6; Mum does an alarm drizzle as a favour. Clocks just say "now" and "later".
- **Competitive lying down**: he reached county level, came fourth in the regionals, and came second in a final against Brindle (he sat up to check on him).
- **Kevin** remembers him, whatever people say about goldfish. **Barry** is a monstera.
